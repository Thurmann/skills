# Travel Vlog

Use this skill for a multi-day travel film made from camera footage. Begin with the `/hyperframes` skill, then route a longer custom film through `/general-video`. Use the relevant HyperFrames core, CLI, and media-use skills before authoring compositions, rendering, or changing media.

Before resuming an existing production, read its `AGENTS.md`, authoritative brief/prompt, and checkpoint. Project instructions and the user's latest confirmed facts override these reusable defaults. Treat original media as read-only. Keep the approved storyboard, EDL, project, render manifest, logs, and status files together so the film can be resumed and reviewed.

Read the references as needed:

- [Production flow](references/production-flow.md) for discovery, approval gates, handoff, and milestone tracking.
- [Editorial and framing](references/editorial-and-framing.md) for pacing, source sound, cards, and the no-zoom rule.
- [Render reliability](references/render-reliability.md) before proxy preparation, long renders, retries, or final concatenation.
- [Japan week 5–6 facts](references/japan-week-5-6.md) only when resuming that specific project; never carry those dates or locations into another trip.

For each production, adapt the plan to its actual footage and user-confirmed itinerary. Do not reuse an earlier week's date range, destinations, clip list, or runtime as facts. Mark uncertainty rather than inventing locations. Do not start HyperFrames composition construction until the required storyboard/EDL approval in the project instructions has been obtained.

For complex productions, keep reviews independent and scoped to one concrete artifact. If the workspace requires GPT-6-Luna reviews, follow that model/delegation rule; request reviews after the storyboard, after the complete preview, and before final MP4 approval. Save review reports under the production's `production-state/reviews/`. If the required reviewer is unavailable, document that and continue with the remaining QA.

Do not declare delivery complete until the final MP4, QA evidence, and required editable project files are verified. Preserve successful render segments until final review and clean only documented, regenerable intermediates afterward.
